## 🔥 Top Stories

- **Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1 — the same frontier model at two safeguard levels, ~25% cheaper for typical workloads.** Announced Sep 2, Fable 5.1 is generally available and billed as Anthropic's most advanced model for coding and knowledge work (world's most advanced for coding, and extends long-running problem-solving); this follows Monday's Fable 5 release. Mythos 5.1 is the same model under stronger safeguards, available only through trusted-access programs, with an access program developed with the US government for advanced biology capabilities (enrollment opening soon). On Terminal-Bench 4.0 it scores 55.8% vs 42.0% for Fable 5, and 52.6% on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 (more than double Fable 5). Pricing: ~25% lower than Fable 5 for typical workloads, up to ~45% for highly agentic work (via a big cut in cache-read pricing). Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS) gives customers zero data retention with data stored in customer-controlled cloud (rolling out in phases this fall; eligible customers can use Fable 5.1 with zero data retention until then). Cybersecurity safeguards now block 60% fewer false positives; Fable 5.1 can discover software vulnerabilities but not develop exploits. On Claude/Claude Code now (requires extra usage credits); also on AWS today, Perplexity Computer (Pro/Max), OpenRouter, and Nous Portal (20% off). ## 📄 New Research Papers

Fresh picks from arXiv cs.AI | cs.LG | cs.CL | cs.CV (Sep 1 submissions):

1. **Efficient SWE Agent Benchmarking via Trajectory-Aware Evaluation** ([arxiv.org/abs/2609.01603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01603)) — cuts the cost of evaluating software-engineering agents by leveraging trajectories instead of running full multi-step tasks.
2. **CordisBench: Can Language Models Reason About Component Lifecycles in Dynamic Agent Harnesses?** ([arxiv.org/abs/2609.01600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01600)) — a benchmark probing whether LLMs can reason about the plugins/software that shape their own execution as harnesses become dynamic.
3. **The Rise of Verbal Reinforcement Learning** ([arxiv.org/abs/2609.01597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01597)) — argues natural language is becoming a primary feedback channel for improving language agents.
4. **Mechanism Design for Alignment and Control** ([arxiv.org/abs/2609.01595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01595)) — a framework for mechanism design with AI agents whose preferences and capabilities are unknown.
5. **Facet-0: A Robotic Foundation Model for Contact-Rich Precise Manipulation** ([arxiv.org/abs/2609.01596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01596)) — a robotic foundation model targeting fine, contact-rich manipulation.
6. **The Structure of Quantization Damage in LLMs: Why the Next Bit Should Be Spent Globally** ([arxiv.org/abs/2609.01587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01587)) — how quantization degrades LLMs and why precision should be allocated globally rather than per-tensor.
7. **Adaptive Critical Token-Aware Retrieval for Repository-Level Code Generation** ([arxiv.org/abs/2609.01601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01601)) — token-aware retrieval that improves repository-scale code generation.
8. **Beyond Scores: Understanding LLM-as-a-Judge Mechanisms in Summarization Evaluation** ([arxiv.org/abs/2609.01604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01604)) — unpacks the internal procedure by which LLM judges evaluate summarization quality.
9. **StudentSim: Training LLM-based Student Simulators** ([arxiv.org/abs/2609.01591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01591)) — simulating learners so AI tutors can adapt to individual students.
10. **Scaling Near-Optimal SFT-RL Annotation Budget Allocation from Small to Large LLMs** ([arxiv.org/abs/2609.01573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01573)) — how to allocate annotation budgets between SFT and RL as models scale.
